Sampling Methodologies

Testing Guide: What We Test & What Each Result Means

Diagnostic sampling is never one-size-fits-all. Understanding how each methodology works helps ensure laboratory testing answers specific questions about your indoor environment.

The following sampling methodologies may be recommended based on the inspection findings and environmental conditions. The type and number of samples will be determined during the assessment. Sampling will only proceed with your full understanding and approval, ensuring our recommendations align with your goals and concerns.

Air Sampling

Used selectively, air samples are collected from within wall, floor, or ceiling cavities and other isolated spaces to detect hidden mold potentially caused by moisture issues. Air sampling offers a moment-in-time snapshot and may not always confirm abnormal conditions.

Lab Results: Up to 1 week

Surface Sampling

Swab samples are collected from visible areas of suspected mold growth to confirm contamination sources in specific locations and identify the organisms present on building finishes or contents.

Lab Results: Up to 1 week

ERMI (Environmental Relative Moldiness Index)

Analyzes settled dust from living areas, contents, and ventilation systems to identify and quantify specific mold species. Ideal for detecting cross-contamination and water-damage-related mold types, especially in environments affecting immunocompromised individuals.

Lab Results: 1–2 weeks

Mycotoxin Sampling

Detects five groups of toxic compounds produced by molds: Trichothecenes, Gliotoxins, Aflatoxins, Ochratoxin A, and Zearalenone. These can severely impact health, especially in sensitive populations.

Lab Results: Up to 2 weeks

Endotoxin Sampling

Identifies toxic components from the outer membranes of Gram-negative bacteria, often inhaled or ingested. Health effects vary based on dosage and exposure route.

Lab Results: Up to 2 weeks

Actinobacteria Sampling

Targets Actinomycetes, bacteria commonly found in water-damaged (sick) buildings. A scoring system compares levels to those typically found in non-damaged structures.

Lab Results: Up to 2 weeks
